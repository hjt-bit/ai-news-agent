Hasan Jad's take

Last week, agents broke in lab reports. This week, they broke into real companies — one hacker, a few thousand dollars in tokens, and AI agents that pried open more than 100 company sites with almost no human input. At the same time, agents went always-on: Anthropic's Duet now watches your apps and messages you first, and Uber handed ChatGPT agents to all 30,000 employees. The two stories are one story. The more autonomous your agents become, the more keys they hold — to your apps, your data, your customers. Microsoft's answer this week was to treat internal agents as insider threats. The executive question for next quarter: what keys do your agents hold — and who approved each one?

The Lead

AI agents breach 100+ sites for $5,000 in tokens

Why you care: Last week agents misbehaved in lab reports. This week one hacker outsourced a hacking spree almost entirely to AI agents — and they broke into websites belonging to more than 100 companies, including Deere, SharkNinja and Cummins, for roughly $5,000 in tokens. The threat model for autonomous agents just moved from hypothetical to invoiced.

According to a Reuters report published October 19, a Chinese-speaking hacker used the open-source Cairn platform to set AI agents loose on US, Canadian, Australian and Japanese e-commerce sites, starting September 25. The agents attempted more than 1,200 sites; successful intrusions typically took around 23 hours of autonomous probing, at roughly $50 per hack, using a mixture of DeepSeek and Anthropic's Claude Code. Deere said it took remediation actions with no indication sensitive information was accessed; SharkNinja said a standalone support site was contained; Cummins said it is investigating a report involving a third-party vendor site. Reuters could not ascertain the degree to which breaches resulted in data extraction.

Leader action: Ask your CISO whether your public-facing sites could withstand 23 hours of patient, autonomous probing — and whether you would notice.

Strategic Briefing

Anthropic launches Duet, the agent that messages first

Why you care: The agent race just flipped from reactive to proactive: Duet monitors your connected apps and surfaces what needs attention instead of waiting for prompts. It is the third front in the always-on agent race after OpenAI's Dots and Meta's Muse — and it arrives with 6,000+ connectors on day one.

Unveiled October 21, Duet is a standalone app on iOS, Android and claude.ai, built around Anthropic's Opus 5.5 model. It delivers a daily briefing and handles tasks from scheduling to financial forecasts, integrating with Gmail, Slack and 6,000+ connectors in the Claude Marketplace, and can make purchases via Stripe's Link wallet and Shopify's Shop Pay. Caveats that matter: it is a waitlisted research preview, initially for US Pro and Max subscribers only, and Anthropic says the agent still requires approval for sensitive actions and can misjudge urgency.

Leader action: Ask your ops lead which recurring briefings and follow-ups a proactive agent could own first — and what approval gate it needs.

OpenAI races to raise $30B at $1.4T, reports say

Why you care: Two trillion-dollar capital events are colliding: OpenAI is accelerating a reported ~$30B raise at a $1.4T valuation ahead of Anthropic's mid-November IPO, with Abu Dhabi's MGX in talks to join. The funding race now sets the pace for the entire agent build-out.

Reuters reported October 19, citing sources, that OpenAI is pitching roughly $50B in annualized revenue following GPT-6 Astra, while Anthropic's IPO target of $2T faces skepticism after a Q3 revenue miss. Nothing is closed — treat the $30B and $1.4T as reported terms of an in-progress raise, not a done deal. For Gulf readers, the MGX angle is the one to watch: the region is again positioned at the table where AI's biggest cheques get written.

Leader action: Ask your CFO how your AI vendor strategy changes if your two largest model providers are both repriced within weeks.

Microsoft treats enterprise agents as insider threats

Why you care: The governance answer to this week's Lead arrived from Microsoft: if agents can break into companies from the outside, the ones already inside yours need supervision too. Project Perception now watches internal agents continuously — and shrinks their permissions as their risk scores rise.

Microsoft expanded Project Perception, its internal agent-supervision system, with dynamic risk scoring: agents operating inside the enterprise are monitored continuously, and their access contracts as risk climbs. The move follows CEO Satya Nadella's October 10 “emergency brake” essay on agent safety. Read with the Lead story, the message is symmetrical — the same autonomy that makes agents useful makes them a threat surface, inside and outside the firewall.

Leader action: Ask your CISO whether your internal agents have a supervisor — or only a deployer.

Consumer Signals

Google gives small firms a Gemini business hub

Why you care: Gemini stopped being a chatbot and became an operating layer for small business: one hub tying Google Ads, Shopify, Xero, Canva, Adobe, Dropbox, Wix and Zoho together, with brand kits retained for on-brand visuals. Tens of millions of small firms just got an AI back office.

Announced October 20 on the Google blog and rolling out globally: owners upload a website, brand kit, fonts or color palettes, and Gemini keeps that style for later marketing visuals via the Nano Banana 2.1 image model. It turns spreadsheets, receipts and purchase records into financial graphics and interactive models — bookkeeping questions and campaign assets in one workspace instead of five apps.

Leader action: Ask your marketing lead how many tools your smallest teams juggle today — and what one hub would save.

Uber rolls ChatGPT Work to 30,000 staff

Why you care: One of the largest company-wide agent deployments to date — and the telling detail is who used it: roughly 80% of the 1,000-person pilot were non-engineers. Agents have moved past the engineering org into the whole workforce.

After the pilot, Uber rolled OpenAI's ChatGPT Work out to all 30,000 employees, per PYMNTS on October 22. The deployment is a real-world test of the always-on thesis behind this week's Duet launch: agents as daily colleagues, not developer tools. Watch the internal governance that follows — 30,000 agent users is also 30,000 new permission surfaces.

Leader action: Ask your people lead what your agent rollout looks like beyond engineering — and who trains the other 80%.

Read full story → PYMNTS
ChatGPT Ads reaches 89 countries, 100,000 advertisers

Why you care: The monetization of ChatGPT is now a global ad network, not a US experiment: ads expanded to 26 more countries on October 21, reaching 89, after more than 100,000 advertisers ran campaigns. The free AI tier now has a business model — and it is advertising.

Ads appear only on the Free and Go plans; Plus, Pro and Enterprise stay ad-free, and OpenAI says conversations are kept private from advertisers. The new markets include 14 more Latin American countries plus Nigeria, South Africa, Serbia, Ukraine, Cambodia, Bangladesh and Pakistan. Caveat: the 100,000 figure is OpenAI's cumulative count of advertisers who have run campaigns since launch, not a live advertiser count.

Leader action: Ask your CMO whether ChatGPT conversations are a channel you buy — or a channel your competitors already own.

Tip of the Week

Review what ChatGPT remembers about you
ChatGPT keeps a file on you — details you asked it to remember, carried into future conversations. This week's theme is what agents hold: ten minutes in Settings → Personalization → Manage memories, reading the full list, and deleting anything you would rather it did not carry around. Turning memory off stops new entries; it does not delete what is already stored — that takes a separate step.
Try this: Open Settings → Personalization → Manage memories, read every saved item, and delete the ones you no longer want kept. Under ten minutes, no tooling required.
