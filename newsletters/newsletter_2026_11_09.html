The Lead

Anthropic's amended IPO filing reveals $895B compute bill

Why you care: The IPO story got a second, bigger chapter: Anthropic's amended filing discloses roughly $895 billion in committed compute spend — the demand signal behind every data-centre build in the Gulf. Nine-month revenue hit $33.2 billion. But the filing still names no price, share count, or listing date.

Amendment No. 1 to Form S-1, filed November 2, 2026: purchase commitments and leases totalling approximately $895.4 billion, substantially all of it compute and infrastructure — $447.5 billion expected before the end of 2030, and $69.8 billion due within 12 months. Nine-month revenue reached $33.2 billion. The price range, share count, and listing date are still blank; Anthropic has applied to list on Nasdaq under the ticker ANT. The reported $2 trillion valuation and $100 billion-plus raise from last week remain reported targets, not terms in the filing.

Leader action: Ask your CFO what a public $895 billion compute bill does to your AI budget assumptions — and to the price of the capacity you're buying.

Strategic Briefing

Marvell buys Lumentum for $115B in all-stock optics bet

Why you care: The optics layer under every Gulf AI data centre just consolidated: Marvell agreed to buy Lumentum in an all-stock deal worth about $115 billion, betting the AI build-out runs on light, not copper.

A definitive agreement announced November 2: 4.500 Marvell shares for each Lumentum share, an implied enterprise value of about $115 billion (about $1,132 per share, a 23.9% premium to Lumentum's October 28 close). Marvell holders would own about 67% of the combined company, Lumentum holders about 33%. The combined addressable market: $550 billion, with more than $1 billion in annual run-rate cost synergies and accretion to non-GAAP earnings per share in year one. Close is expected in the first half of 2028, subject to shareholder votes and regulatory approvals — an agreement, not a closed deal.

Leader action: Ask your infrastructure lead how many suppliers your optical connectivity depends on — and what consolidation does to your pricing.

Kimi K3.1 Pro open weights: 2.8T parameters, 1.56TB download

Why you care: A near-frontier open model any team can self-host: Moonshot released Kimi K3.1 Pro's weights — 2.8 trillion parameters, a 1-million-token context, scoring 65.7% on Terminal-Bench 4.0 against Claude Opus 5.5's 66.4%. The catch is the licence, not the download.

Weights released November 4 (announced October 23): a Mixture-of-Experts model with 2.8T total parameters and 104B activated per token (896 experts, 16 per token), a 1,048,576-token context, and MXFP4 weights. The download is about 1.56 TB across 96 files — a multi-GPU server model, not a laptop model. Moonshot's own figures put it at 65.7% on Terminal-Bench 4.0, against 66.4% for Claude Opus 5.5 and 58.2% for GPT-6 Astra. The licence: a separate agreement with Moonshot is required if model-as-a-service revenue exceeds $20 million over any 12 months; internal use is exempt.

Leader action: Ask your platform lead whether a self-hosted open model fits your data-residency needs — and whether the $20M service clause affects your plans.

OpenAI's GPT-6 Cyber finds 948 Linux exploits, rated Critical

Why you care: Offensive AI capability just jumped a tier: GPT-6 Cyber scored 48.5% on ExploitGym, against 32.1% for the previous model, and developed 948 working Linux kernel exploits — and OpenAI rated it Critical in cybersecurity, its highest tier. All capability claims are OpenAI's own.

Announced November 5: 48.5% on ExploitGym, up from 32.1% for GPT-5.6 Cyber (a 51% relative improvement); 948 Linux kernel privilege-escalation exploits developed, from 1,724 vulnerabilities reproduced, against 166 by the previous model; and 81 previously unknown high-severity Firefox vulnerabilities, classified by Mozilla, with fixes landing. The first model rated Critical in cybersecurity under OpenAI's Preparedness Framework, up from High. Access is restricted to approved Daybreak Red customers via API and Codex, alongside a $1 billion Daybreak for Frontline Defenders grant programme.

Leader action: Ask your CISO to assume adversaries get the same tooling — and whether your defensive AI access matches the threat.

Abu Dhabi signs four OpenAI deals across energy and government

Why you care: The region's biggest energy company and its government are now running frontier AI on sovereign UAE infrastructure — the template other Gulf states will be measured against.

Announced November 2: Crown Prince Sheikh Khaled bin Mohamed bin Zayed met Sam Altman and witnessed four agreements. ADNOC, with G42 (via Inception42) and OpenAI, becomes the first energy company to deploy OpenAI's frontier technology, on GPT models with in-country inference on UAE GPUs. Abu Dhabi Government and OpenAI launched the world's first AI-native Machine-Readable Government initiative, led by the Department of Government Enablement, with a first pilot already in production at the Abu Dhabi Agriculture and Food Safety Authority on food imports. Mubadala, Inception42, and OpenAI signed on investment analysis; Inception42 and OpenAI signed a five-year agreement for frontier AI adoption across UAE government, enterprise, and education. No contract values were disclosed.

Leader action: Ask your government-affairs lead what an AI-native, machine-readable government means for your regulatory filings.

Altman: 10,000 agents of next GPT worked four days

Why you care: OpenAI's CEO chose Abu Dhabi to preview its next model — and to name energy as AI's binding constraint. The Gulf's leverage in the AI race, stated on its own stage. One caveat: The National notes the headline claim could not immediately be verified independently.

Speaking at the ADIPEC 2026 opening on November 2, Altman said 10,000 agents of the next GPT model worked about four days — with no hierarchy — on what he described as a Millennium Prize proof, and that the model “is going to be released soon” — no name, no date. The heaviest AI user now consumes about 500 billion tokens a month, against 100,000 six years ago, and energy was his one-word answer to the challenge he faces. The underlying proof is not yet independently verified and credit is disputed.

Leader action: Ask your energy lead what AI's power demand means for your data-centre plans — energy is now the binding constraint, not chips.

Consumer Signals

ChatGPT Images 2.6 adds GIFs, 50% faster generation

Why you care: Every leader's social and marketing visuals just got a motion layer: ChatGPT can now animate an image into a looping GIF, and generates images 50% faster.

Launched November 4: GIF creation from images — animate a mascot, product shot, or sketch into a loop — plus 50% faster image generation, API models GPT-Image-2.6 Flare (fast, about 6 seconds at low quality) and Sunburst (detail and consistency), saved reference images, and 4 billion images a week now created across ChatGPT, Codex, and the API. Available globally in ChatGPT and Codex across all tiers, on web, desktop, and mobile. One caveat: the GIF rollout was still reaching accounts on November 5, so some readers may not see it yet.

Leader action: Ask your marketing lead which product shot becomes your first GIF — thirty seconds, no designer.

Claude's Personal Finance beta links US bank accounts

Why you care: The AI assistant that reads your bank has arrived — but not here: a US-only beta with no regional timeline, so Gulf leaders watch the gap (and any leader with US accounts can use it now).

Announced November 5: a beta on the Pro ($20), Max ($100), and Ultra ($200) plans in the US, rolling out in waves. Read-only bank and card linking via Plaid — Claude never sees your password — with up to 24 months of transactions, and no ability to move, manage, or invest your money. No ads, and hub chats are not used to train models. Anthropic's own example: $4,309.37 in monthly spend, $1,024.51 of it on food and drink. Anthropic's own caveat: “Claude can make mistakes in its answers, so ask which transactions are behind a number, and check important figures with your bank.”

Leader action: If you hold US accounts, link one and ask Claude where your money went this month — then check its math against your bank.
