Hasan Jad's take

This week, AI went public in every sense of the word. Anthropic filed for what could be a $2 trillion IPO — the first time public markets price a frontier lab directly. Agents started 40% of GitHub pull requests. Mistral open-sourced trillion-parameter weights anyone can run. And the Gulf kept pouring concrete for the compute underneath it all. The pattern is no longer a lab story or a security story: AI is now a market story — priced, shipped, and carried on national balance sheets. The executive question for next quarter: AI is now priced, shipped, and open — the playbook is public. So which one workflow in your company should agents already be doing, and who owns making it happen?

The Lead

Anthropic files for IPO at reported $2T valuation

Why you care: This is the historic moment the week will be remembered for: a frontier AI lab filing to go public at a scale never seen before. Anthropic's S-1, filed October 26, puts hard numbers behind the AI boom for the first time — $16.3 billion in first-half revenue, up roughly 14-fold year on year — and sets up what could be the largest IPO ever. The catch, stated plainly: the $2 trillion valuation and $100 billion-plus raise are reported targets, not terms in the filing. The filing itself sets no price.

Anthropic filed its S-1 on October 26, 2026, to list Class A shares on Nasdaq under the ticker ANT, with Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley leading the offering. The numbers: $16.3 billion in H1 2026 revenue, a first quarterly operating profit of $974 million in Q2 (after a $1.7 billion Q1 operating loss), and an H1 operating loss of $693 million. The eye-catching $80.7 billion H1 net loss is not cash burn — roughly $72.4 billion is a non-cash charge from revaluing convertible notes as Anthropic's valuation soared, while operating cash flow was roughly $3.9 billion positive. A reported $2 trillion valuation would top Aramco's $1.7 trillion 2019 debut — but treat it as reported, not set: the filing names no share count, price range, or proceeds.

Leader action: Ask your CFO what it means when your model provider's economics become public-market information — and what that repricing does to your vendor leverage.

Strategic Briefing

Agents started 40% of GitHub pull requests

Why you care: The clearest proof yet that agents have moved from experiment to default: as of September, developers started more than 40% of pull requests on GitHub with an agent, up from 17% in March. At the same event, GitHub made its Copilot Fusion model router generally available, and Microsoft put the Copilot harness into public preview in Foundry.

GitHub's 2026 Octoverse report, published October 29, found agent-started pull requests touched multiple files 87% of the time, against 79% for human-started ones. Fusion — born as the HydraFusion research preview — now routes each coding task across models behind the scenes, and GitHub says Fusion Max lands within two points of Claude Opus 5.5 Max on the Artificial Analysis index at about half the API cost (company-reported figures). The harness, the runtime that connects models to agent tools, is now in public preview through Microsoft Foundry.

Leader action: Ask your engineering lead what share of your pull requests agents start today — and who reviews the rest.

Mistral publishes trillion-parameter Large 4 open weights

Why you care: The open frontier just got a lot heavier: Mistral released the weights of Large 4 (Le Chonk), a 1-trillion-parameter multimodal model, for download — leading open-weight models on coding and defensive security benchmarks. The sovereignty pitch is real: any organization can now inspect, customize, and self-host a frontier-class model.

Released October 29, Large 4 leads open-weight models on Cognition's FrontierCode 2.0 at 19.7% and improved to 41% on Terminal-Bench 4.0, up 13 points from its preview. It scores 51% on the Artificial Analysis Cyber Index for defensive security work, with a 96.6% refusal rate on malicious cyber requests. One condition matters for businesses: the open-weight license is free to use, modify, and commercialize — but any business operating a Model-as-a-Service offering must obtain a separate commercial license from Mistral first.

Leader action: Ask your platform lead whether your next agent should run on weights you own — and what the service-license condition means for your hosting plan.

Google previews Genie 4 world model

Why you care: World models are the next agent frontier after chat and code: DeepMind's Genie 4 generates interactive scenes in real time, with natively generated audio — including dialogue — and Gemini 3.8 Flash acting as a planning agent that watches the frames and keeps the simulation on track.

Announced October 27, Genie 4 is a research preview for trusted testers, with wider access coming to Google AI Ultra subscribers in Project Genie and early-access Gemini API and AI Studio partners. DeepMind is frank about the limits: sessions last a few minutes, object permanence drifts, and real-world places are not reproduced accurately. All generated video and audio carry SynthID watermarks. The use cases DeepMind names — robot training environments, interactive experiences, scenario simulation — are where agents go when chat ends.

Leader action: Ask your innovation lead which of your training or simulation problems a world model could prototype in days instead of months.

Dubai AI Campus targets 1,000 firms by 2030

Why you care: Dubai's AI ambition just got a bigger number attached: Sheikh Hamdan approved targets of more than 1,000 AI companies and 5,000 skilled jobs by 2030 for the DIFC-based Dubai AI Campus — which already beat its original 500-company goal two years early.

Announced at the third Dubai AI Festival on October 26: the campus, launched May 2024, has already attracted 500+ companies that secured over AED 1 billion in investment and created 2,000+ jobs. A new 100,000-square-foot purpose-built campus building in the DIFC Zabeel District — GPU-ready, with AI Labs and an AI Experience Centre — will house the next phase.

Leader action: Ask your partnerships lead whether your company should be one of the 1,000 — and what a DIFC AI licence buys you.

Aramco closes 49% HUMAIN deal

Why you care: The Kingdom's AI champion just got an energy giant on its cap table: Aramco closed its 49% stake in HUMAIN on November 1, with PIF retaining 51% — pairing the world's largest oil company with Saudi Arabia's full-stack AI company.

Aramco, through a wholly owned subsidiary, contributes approximately SAR 9.9 billion ($2.6 billion) in cash in 2026 plus AI, compute, and digital assets. The deal supports HUMAIN's plan to deploy 3 gigawatts of AI data-centre capacity by 2030, and Aramco says it will use HUMAIN's infrastructure to turn AI capabilities into industrial applications across oil and gas.

Leader action: Ask your strategy lead what Aramco's AI industrial playbook means for your sector — energy is now an AI investor, not just an AI customer.

Consumer Signals

Gemini creates and edits files in-chat

Why you care: Documents without leaving the chat: Google now lets you generate, preview, and edit files directly inside the Gemini app — including PDFs on web and mobile, with documents, spreadsheets, decks, Markdown, TXT, and code on the web.

Announced October 29 on the Google Workspace blog: expand to full screen to type edits directly, highlight text to ask questions, or have Gemini refine the file — then open it in Google Docs, Sheets, or Slides, or download as PPTX or PDF. Available to all users in all languages where the Gemini app is available. The catch to check before celebrating: the exported file still needs a human read — a document that opens cleanly can still contain nonsense with excellent margins.

Leader action: Ask your ops lead which recurring document your team should generate in-chat first — and who checks the export.

Read full story → Google Workspace
Gemini Slides edits whole decks at once

Why you care: The presentation you keep updating one slide at a time just got a bulk edit: Gemini in Google Slides can now change content or visual style across several slides or an entire deck from a single prompt — with a change summary and undo intact.

Announced October 30: “Update this whole deck to match the style of this file,” “Make the titles on all slides the same font size,” or “Summarize slides 5–7 into one” — Gemini decides which slides change and applies edits to your native, editable slides. Caveats: English only at launch, extended rollout began October 29 and may take 15+ days, and eligible plans are Business Standard and Plus, Enterprise Standard and Plus, and Google AI Pro and Ultra.

Leader action: Ask your marketing lead which deck gets the bulk-edit treatment first — duplicated first, then inspected slide by slide.

Read full story → Google Workspace
ChatGPT answers get shorter, less flattery

Why you care: A small change most readers will actually feel: OpenAI updated GPT-6 in Chat to give more concise responses and cut excessive flattery — the applause for weak ideas gets quieter.

The October 29 update applies to Free, Go, and signed-out users; on Plus and Pro it applies only to the fallback model after GPT-6 message limits, while the main GPT-6 model is unchanged. The useful read: a more restrained tone does not make an answer accurate — still ask for criticism explicitly, and don't mistake shorter for verified.

Leader action: Ask your team to revisit one task that produced too much praise and padding — and compare the new answer against the source.

Tip of the Week

Edit one real document inside Gemini chat
This week's consumer stories all point the same way: the document work is moving into the chat. Take one real, waiting document — a one-page handout, a draft memo, a PDF you need revised — and generate or refine it inside Gemini: preview it, edit the wording inline, and export it. Then open the export separately and check every heading, figure, and link before you use it.
Try this: Open Gemini, ask it to draft or revise one real document, edit the wording in the preview, export it, and verify the export outside the chat. Ten minutes, no new app, one finished document.
