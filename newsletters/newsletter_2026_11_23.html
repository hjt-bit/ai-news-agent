The Lead

Nvidia doubles revenue, board rules out Huang return

Why you care: Nvidia lost its founder-CEO and doubled its revenue in the same week. Q3 revenue hit $115.8B, up 103%, as the Vera Rubin chip shipped at record pace. On the same earnings call, the board said it does not intend to reinstate Jensen Huang as CEO.

Nvidia reported Q3 FY2027 revenue of $115.8B for the quarter ended October 25, 2026. That is up 103% year over year and 20% sequentially. GAAP net income was $65.3B, up 105%. Data Center revenue was $107.6B, up 110% year over year. Edge Computing revenue was $8.2B, up 42%. GAAP diluted earnings per share were $2.70; non-GAAP were $2.68. Gross margins were 74.8% GAAP and 74.9% non-GAAP. The Q4 FY2027 outlook is revenue of $130.0B, plus or minus 3%, with no Data Center compute revenue from China assumed. Margins are guided at 71.0% to 72.0%. The Vera Rubin NVL72 has shipped. Nvidia calls it “the fastest product ramp in the company's history.” Interim CEO Colette Kress said: “Vera Rubin is off to an explosive start, our roadmap is on track and our ambition has never been greater.” On the earnings call, Kress raised FY2028 revenue-growth guidance to about 75% year over year, from 70%. She said Vera Rubin generated nearly $20B in its first quarter of production shipments. Nvidia returned $28.1B to shareholders in Q3. About $227.3B of buyback authorisation remains. The governance news came on the same call. Board chair Harvey Jones said: “after disclosure of personal behavior inconsistent with the company's code of conduct, standards, and cultural norms, Jensen Huang voluntarily resigned his positions as President, CEO and Board member. The NVIDIA Board unanimously accepted his resignation and does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO.” The board retained executive search firm Heidrick & Struggles to lead the search for a permanent CEO. Kress continues as interim CEO while remaining CFO. The board established a National Security and Export Control Oversight Committee of independent directors, senior external advisers and legal counsel. Earlier the same day, Huang posted his first public statement since resigning: “If the Board of Directors decides that my return is in the best interest of our shareholders, our employees, the company, and America, I would be honored to answer these calls and return as CEO.” He also wrote: “I own my mistakes completely, and I am committed to cooperating with the Board's independent investigation.” Kress closed the call: “Our momentum is real. Our demand is accelerating. Our supply is growing.”

Leader action: Ask your team which of your AI suppliers is still a founder-led bet — and what your plan is if that founder, that vendor, or that export rule changes.

Anthropic closes record $119.5B IPO

Why you care: The first frontier AI lab is now a public company. Anthropic closed a $119.5B IPO — and the chip stack (AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, NVIDIA and TSMC) bought in through a private placement.

Anthropic closed its IPO on November 20, 2026. It sold 1,127,000,000 Class A shares at $83.00 per share. That includes the full exercise of the underwriters' option for 147,000,000 additional shares. Gross proceeds were about $93.5B. About $91.6B went to Anthropic. A concurrent private placement also closed. It sold 313,253,007 Class N shares to AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, NVIDIA and TSMC Partners at $83.00 per share. That raised about $26.0B. Aggregate gross proceeds from the IPO and the private placement were about $119.5B. Shares began trading on the Nasdaq Global Select Market on November 19, 2026, under the symbol “ANT”. The SEC registration was declared effective November 18, 2026. Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley were the lead book-running managers.

Leader action: Ask your procurement lead what listed-lab disclosure means for your AI vendor contracts — and whether your suppliers should meet the same transparency bar.

Washington rescinds AI Diffusion Rule, curbs remote chip access

Why you care: The US just redrew the export-control map for AI chips. The Biden-era Diffusion Rule is gone, replaced by licence requirements on remote access to advanced semiconductors — and on shipments to Singapore, Malaysia and other regional hubs.

The Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security issued an interim final rule on November 19, 2026. It is published in the Federal Register, Vol. 91, No. 222, 91 FR 74970. Comments are due January 19, 2027. It creates a new licence requirement for exports, reexports and transfers of advanced “Super Intelligence” semiconductors to overseas data centres that remotely supply significant compute to companies headquartered in Country Group D:5, which includes China, or Macau. Commercial recommender systems are exempt. It creates a new licence requirement for advanced semiconductors to Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil, which it calls high diversion risk. There is an exclusion for US-headquartered recipients that meet reporting requirements. The rule formally rescinds the Biden-era AI Diffusion Rule of January 15, 2025, which the rule states “has never been enforced”. It also narrows the pathways for front-end chip fabricators and packagers to overcome advanced-node logic-chip presumptions.

Leader action: Ask your legal and infrastructure leads to re-check your export-control exposure — especially any capacity hosted in Singapore or Malaysia, and any remote-access arrangements.

OpenAI's GPT-6.2 gets better and 20% cheaper

Why you care: OpenAI's new frontier model is better at the work leaders actually buy it for — and 20% cheaper. Astra is for hard professional work; Sol is the efficient everyday model. Both live in Work and Codex, not the Chat most people open.

OpenAI launched GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol on November 17. Astra is the new frontier model for complex professional work. Sol is the efficient everyday model. Both deliver better results with fewer output tokens. Astra's API pricing is $8 per million input tokens, $0.40 cached input and $40 output. That is 20% below GPT-6 Astra, with a 60% cached-input discount. Sol keeps GPT-6.1 Sol's rates: $2, $0.10 and $10. OpenAI's own benchmarks, not independently verified: Astra scores 5.2 points above GPT-6 Astra on Agents' Last Exam at under a third of the cost, and 5.5 points above Claude Fable 5.2 at about 5% of the cost. It sets a new high on Terminal-Bench-Science, up 9 points on GPT-6 Astra. It more than doubles Claude Fable 5.2's pass rate on the Harvey legal benchmark (LAB-AA) at about one-sixth the cost. Astra is OpenAI's first externally released model to meet the “High” threshold for AI self-improvement. Both models meet the “Critical” cybersecurity threshold, with tighter safeguards on higher-risk cyber assistance. Availability: ChatGPT Work and Codex for Plus, Pro, Enterprise and Edu users from launch. API IDs are gpt-6.2-astra and gpt-6.2-sol.

Leader action: Ask your team to re-price your heaviest agent workloads on Astra — and to update acceptable-use policies before staff use it for vulnerability work.

HUMAIN launches Arabic-first voice AI platform

Why you care: Saudi Arabia's national AI company shipped a real commercial product: an Arabic-first voice platform that understands Saudi dialects and answers in the caller's own dialect. The API is live.

HUMAIN Voice launched on November 19. It combines automatic speech recognition, text-to-speech and full-duplex voice agents behind a single API, built for customer service and enterprise operations. It is Arabic-first by design: it supports the Saudi dialects Najdi and Hijazi, plus Khaleeji and more than 15 Arabic dialects, English, and natural Arabic–English code-switching mid-sentence. Voice agents reply in the caller's own dialect. Data stays in Saudi Arabia. The models are named Bayan (speech recognition), Nida (8 kHz phone speech recognition) and Nebula (text-to-speech). The hosted voice agent is labelled beta. Pricing is public: a free tier of 5,000 credits, then plans from 49 to 649 SAR a month, about $13 to $173. HUMAIN also announced HUMAIN-Voice-Lite-80M, an 80-million-parameter open-weight speech-recognition model under Apache 2.0, optimised for CPU, GPU and edge use — but it was not yet downloadable on launch day, so treat it as announced, not delivered. No public accuracy figures yet.

Leader action: Ask your customer-service lead to test HUMAIN Voice on your hardest Arabic calls — the dialect handling is the part to verify before you commit.

Dubai approves City AI Agent, switches on 93 government agents

Why you care: Dubai just moved from AI pilots to AI-native government. Crown Prince Hamdan approved a City AI Agent and switched on 93 specialised agents across 85 government entities — with quantified savings attached.

The approvals came on November 18 at the AI Retreat, on the opening day of Dubai Future Week. The City AI Agent will understand residents' needs, execute tasks and coordinate procedures across Dubai government entities through a single seamless experience. No first services, public launch date or build cost were disclosed. Hamdan also launched the Government Resources AI Agents ecosystem: 93 specialised agents covering HR, finance and procurement, running inside the shared Government Resource Planning system across 85 government entities. Dubai Media Office puts numbers on it: the 93 agents are expected to execute more than 34 million tasks a year, save over 812,000 working hours and cut costs by more than AED 151 million. An AI-powered decision-making platform, NxtBI, was also launched. Hamdan's framing: the shift is “from a government that harnesses AI to enhance its services to one that fundamentally redesigns the way it operates around AI capabilities.”

Leader action: Ask your government-affairs lead what Dubai's agent template means for your dealings with government — and which of your own processes an agent could run.

Google launches Gemini 4 Neon for production work

Why you care: Google's new model sits between its hardest and fastest: frontier-level intelligence built for software engineering, knowledge work and science — at a working price.

Google launched Gemini 4 Neon on November 18. It sits between Carbon, for the hardest long-horizon work, and Hydrogen, for fast everyday work. It offers frontier-level intelligence with fast end-to-end task completion for software engineering, knowledge work and science. Pricing is $3 per million input tokens, $0.15 cached, a 95% discount, and $15 per million output tokens. It has a 1-million-token context window and four thinking levels. Google's own benchmarks, not independently verified: number 3 overall on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, 61 at xhigh; number 2 on the Vals Index at 70.9%; number 1 on the Legal Research Bench and Tax Agent Bench; number 1 on the Artificial Analysis Coding Agent Index at 58.6; number 1 on AutomationBench at 56.3% and Vending-Bench 2 at $16,843. It costs 32% less per task than Carbon at default high thinking, and roughly 50 to 80% less than top competing frontier models. Availability: rolling out now in the Gemini API and Google AI Studio, Gemini for business, Google Antigravity (Pro and Ultra) and the Gemini app (Pro and Ultra).

Leader action: Ask your engineering lead to benchmark Neon against your current coding model on a real task — and to price the difference.

Turn your notes into a shareable site with Gemini Notebook
Gemini Notebook can now turn your sources into an interactive site. Drop in this week's meeting notes, a report and a few links, then ask it to make you a site — a project tracker, a dashboard, a briefing page. It takes about five minutes with material you already have. Sharing works by link; viewers need a Google Account and access to the notebook.
Try this: Open notebook.google.com, start a notebook, add a few sources (meeting notes, a report, links), then type “make me a site” in the notebook chat and describe what you want. Hit “Open in a new tab” to use it, or share the link.
